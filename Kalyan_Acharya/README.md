## What changed after the dataset search

The first proposed predictors required historical valuations, shots, possession and squad information that was not supplied. A public ready-made feature table was inspected but rejected: its generator substitutes default first-match and missing-statistics values. The revised predictors use genuine earlier international scores, match dates and tournament participation, plus host-country context. The group now has reproducible feature calculations rather than manually entered unsupported values.

Every task has exactly eight explanatory variables. **Exactly four underlying predictor concepts are shared**: prior-ten goals scored, prior-ten goals conceded, prior-ten win rate and days since the last eligible match. Metadata such as IDs, dates and team names never enters either regression. See DATA_GUIDE.md for definitions, units and rationale.

## Score and timing conventions — read before submitting

The revised targets are **final goals including extra time, excluding penalty shootouts**. This matches the historical source's definition. It differs from the earlier package's provisional 90-minute convention. The screenshots do not settle this choice; confirm it with the instructor. Do not label these results as 90-minute-score analysis.

The source snapshots were downloaded on 10 October 2026, after the tournament. Predictors are retrospective reconstructions restricted to earlier results; they are not a set of archived predictions published before kickoff. Date-only historical games become eligible only after `match date + 2 UTC days`, strictly before target kickoff. Training labels use a conservative `kickoff + 24 hours` eligibility bound. Host venue/country context is assumed known a week ahead. These are explicit conservative timing assumptions, **not actual first-publication timestamps**. Verify any rescheduling, unusually delayed results or retrospective source corrections before relying on them.

## Model and validation

- All 104 matches remain in the prepared Task 2.1 dataset; both sides of each match form Task 2.2's 208 rows.
- Hold out the last 20% of distinct UTC match days. The resulting test partition has **7 matches / 14 team rows**, from 10–19 July. The training partition has **96 matches / 192 team rows**. One 9 July match is purged from training because the conservative 24-hour label bound is later than the first held-out kickoff. Its record remains in the dataset.
- Within the training period, five expanding forward-validation blocks follow a warm-up of at least 20 eligible matches. Both rows from the same match stay together, and all fitted outcomes precede each validation block.
- Fit median imputation and z-score scaling separately within every training partition, followed by ordinary least-squares linear regression. No feature expansion, automatic variable deletion or holdout-driven model selection is used. The actual sourced predictors have no missing cells.
- Compare with a training-mean baseline. Metrics are MAE, MSE, RMSE, NRMSE (training target range denominator) and R². Training adjusted R² is a diagnostic, not held-out performance.
- Save original-unit coefficients and the full equation. Statsmodels provides HC3 standard errors for Task 2.1 and match-clustered standard errors for Task 2.2. Repeated teams across matches remain a limitation.

## Results to review

See `RESULTS_REVIEW.md` and `results/INTEGRATION_SUMMARY.md`. On the seven-match holdout, the goal-difference model beats the training-mean baseline on RMSE. The team-goals model has a slightly lower MAE but a worse RMSE than its baseline. Report this mixed outcome honestly; do not call both models successful. A seven-match holdout is small and later tournament stages differ from the earlier training matches.

Each task's result folder includes fold metrics/predictions, holdout metrics/predictions, split IDs, model JSON, full equation, robust OLS summary, coefficient inference, missingness, descriptive statistics, correlation matrix, VIF, influence measures, and four plots. JSON coefficients use original units after median imputation; inference CSV coefficients use standardized predictors. Fractional/negative goal predictions are retained for regression evaluation, not clipped or rounded.
